A BSTRACT
Work agents operate over digital artifacts to execute professional knowledgeintensive work, requiring training environments that support long-horizon interaction and trustworthy verification. However, hand-crafted environments incur
prohibitive engineering overhead that prevents environment scaling, whereas synthesis methods sacrifice workspace complexity, realism, or grounded verifiability.
To bridge this gap, we introduce W ORK F ORGE, a scalable synthesis framework
for constructing verifiable work-agent environments from real-world resources.
Starting from expert workflows, W ORK F ORGE first identifies the resources, decisions, and deliverables required by each workflow. It then retrieves relevant
real-world files and organizes them into a workspace. W ORK F ORGE inspects the
workspace to extract concrete, checkable facts about its content. These factual
anchors fix which task types the workspace can support and how their outcomes
can be verified. Therefore, W ORK F ORGE derives each task’s instructions, solution plan, and complementary programmatic and semantic verifiers directly from
these factual anchors, keeping verification traceable to observable workspace evidence. Furthermore, we construct 16.7K verifiable environments across 40 professional domains, with workspaces collectively covering 60 file types. Post-training
Qwen3.5-35B-A3B-Base improves GDPval from 45.5 to 73.6 and APEX Score
from 5.0 to 21.3, while enabling Qwen3.5-27B to achieve highly competitive performance and outperform strong competitors. Our analyses confirm the efficacy
of the proposed method and reveal consistent scaling behaviors across both data
volume and interaction horizons.

I NTRODUCTION

Autonomous agents mark a fundamental paradigm shift in large language models, transitioning
foundation models from passive conversational assistants to active digital workers capable of executing complex, multi-step professional knowledge work (Drouin et al., 2024; Boisvert et al., 2024;
Huang et al., 2024; Patwardhan et al., 2026; Vidgen et al., 2026). Agent assistants such as Claude
Code and Codex illustrate this paradigm through iterative exploration, tool orchestration, and execution over digital workspaces (Anthropic, 2025; OpenAI, 2025).
Unlike conversational or API-calling routines, professional workflows center on concrete deliverables: users specify objectives, and the solution path is embedded within the workspace (Tang et al.,
2026). Producing these deliverables requires agents to untangle multiple file dependencies and make
decisions in authentic workspace artifacts (Zhou et al., 2024; Trivedi et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2026a;
Xi et al., 2026a). Cultivating agents capable of such long-horizon workflow fundamentally demands
agent environments that couple authentic operational friction with trustworthy, verifiable supervision
(Dou et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2026; Xi et al., 2026b).

Figure 1: Comparison of synthetic and realistic workspaces. Realistic workspaces are substantially more complex: they contain more files, span a broader range of file types, and distribute
task-relevant evidence throughout the directory hierarchy. In contrast, synthetic workspaces are
highly curated, with most task-relevant information concentrated in a small set of identifiable files.

While expert-curated environments offer high fidelity, their prohibitive overhead severely impedes
environment scaling (Zhou et al., 2024; Trivedi et al., 2024).
Recent work addresses this bottleneck through automated environment synthesis (Song et al., 2026;
Tu et al., 2026; Cheng et al., 2026; Fan et al., 2026; Zhao et al., 2026; Bai et al., 2026). However,
these methods rely on synthetic environments or task-specific mock resources (Song et al., 2026;
Tu et al., 2026; Bai et al., 2026), yielding toy workspaces with limited file diversity and cross-file
dependencies. This reduces the exploration demands that characterize real professional work in
Figure 1. Likewise, evaluation criteria not grounded in observable workspace facts risk introducing hallucinated or otherwise unsupported requirements, leading to unreliable judges (Zhang et al.,
2026b; Shi et al., 2026).
To address this challenge, we introduce W ORK F ORGE, a scalable framework for constructing complex, verifiable work-agent environments from real-world digital resources. W ORK F ORGE is motivated by a simple premise: professional tasks and their verification are inseparable from the
workspaces in which they are performed. Workspace determines what an agent can explore, while
observable workspace facts determine what can be asked and reliably verified. To operationalize this
premise, W ORK F ORGE formalizes expert workflows as task blueprints, retrieves relevant resources,
and profiles the resulting workspaces to recover file roles, dependencies, and factual anchors. These
anchors jointly condition task generation and verifier design, keeping evaluation traceable to the
workspace: programmatic checks verify deterministic requirements, while semantic rubrics assess
open-ended deliverables against the same facts. Autonomous solvers then execute these tasks to
yield long-horizon trajectories for post-training.
W ORK F ORGE instantiates 16.7K verifiable environments across 40 professional domains, with
workspaces comprising real artifacts, rich file types and cross-file dependencies. Fine-tuning
Qwen3.5-35B-A3B-Base (Qwen Team, 2026) on the resulting trajectories improves performance
across five benchmarks, including gains of 28.1 points on GDPval and 16.3 points on APEX. Controlled studies display significant benefits with increasing volume and trajectory length, and confirm
the efficacy of the proposed method. Our contributions are as follows:

W ORK F ORGE

To scale agent environments without compromising authenticity or verifiability, we introduce
W ORK F ORGE, a framework for translating professional workflows into realistic, verifiable agent
environments. W ORK F ORGE first distills domain expertise into reusable workflow specifications.
It then instantiates each specification as a workspace populated with realistic artifacts and analyzes
the resulting workspace evidence to ground both task construction and verification. Agents execute
these tasks to produce scored trajectories for training. Figure 2 summarizes this four-stage pipeline.
2.1

W ORKFLOW S PECIFICATION

To generate sophisticated professional tasks, we engage domain experts to distill their workflows
into reusable Scene Blueprints, as these workflows identify dependencies between initial conditions,
domain distinctive operations, and final deliverables. We define a collection of M blueprints as

For instance, a teacher can distill the workflow of testing students on a unit and providing targeted explanations into a scene blueprint. The blueprint specifies how to compare student responses against
a standard answer and use teaching materials to group errors. It thus serves as a reusable seed for

Each blueprint serves as a seed that connects expert intent to task generation. Its resource requirements guide source acquisition. Simultaneously, its workflow, decision points, and deliverable guide
task generation using evidence from the instantiated workspace.

E XPERIMENTAL S ETUP

Baselines. We evaluate models trained on W ORK F ORGE-16K against three groups of baselines.
Frontier Proprietary Models: Claude Opus 5.0 (Anthropic, 2026), Claude Opus 4.8, GPT-5.6

Open-source Models (>100B): Qwen3Coder (Qwen Team, 2025), Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (Qwen Team, 2026), DeepSeek-V3.2, DeepSeekV4-Flash, DeepSeek-V4-Pro (DeepSeek-AI et al., 2026), Kimi-K2.5 (Kimi Team, 2026), GLM5.2 (Z.ai, 2026), MiniMax-M3 (MiniMax, 2026b), and MiniMax-M2.7 (MiniMax, 2026a). Benchmarks. We evaluate work-agent capability on five popular benchmarks. WildClawBench (Ding et al., 2026) and Claw-Eval (Ye et al., 2026) emphasize long-horizon execution, multifile interaction, and reliable tool use under repeated trials. GDPval (Patwardhan et al., 2026) and

orchestration, while Agents’ Last Exam (ALE) (Sun et al., 2026) tests extended autonomous problem solving with verifiable outcomes. Additional evaluation details are provided in Appendix C.4.

M AIN R ESULTS

Superior performance of W ORK F ORGE over other baselines. Table 2 reports the capacity of
general agents across state-of-the-art competitors. We make several key observations: (1) W ORK F ORGE delivers consistently competitive results across all benchmarks, establishing strong performance. Notably, W ORK F ORGE-A3B achieves the highest GDPval Score among prior specialized
sub-100B baselines, i.e., 73.6, surpassing the previous SOTA, Clawgym-30A3, by a substantial margin of 38.6 points. Similar trends are observed on WildClaw, ClawEval, APEX, and ALE.
(2) Importantly, these leads do not shrink under strict pass rate: W ORK F ORGE-A3B reaches 12.4%
Pass@1 on ALE, compared with 2.9% of ClawGym-30A3 which rarely complete these challenge tasks. (3) W ORK F ORGE-A3B also delivers a substantial improvement over pretrained model
Qwen3.5-35B-A3B-Base. These gains reflect W ORK F ORGE’s design principle: solving each task
requires the agent to explore complex, real-world file structures and integrate evidence across multiple files into its deliverables. This capability generalizes well on challenging benchmarks.
W ORK F ORGE-27B advances capability toward the frontier. W ORK F ORGE-27B demonstrates
that the supervision produced by our pipeline complements existing agent post-training, improving
Qwen3.5-27B across all five benchmarks, with WildClaw, APEX, and GDPval Scores rising by 8.0,
4.2, and 3.0 points, respectively. Notably, on GDPval, W ORK F ORGE-27B reaches 82.4, surpassing
Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (79.7) despite nearly 15× fewer total parameters, and narrowing the gap to
frontier systems such as Claude Opus 4.8 (89.1) and GPT-5.6 Sol (90.1).

S CALING A NALYSIS

Scaling with Data Volume. To investigate how training data volume affects work-agent performance, we train Qwen3.5-35B-A3B-Base on 1K, 3.6K, 8K, and 16K trajectories under the same
recipe. As shown in Figure 4 ( a), performance improves monotonically with data volume. The
model trained on 16K trajectories achieves scores of 28.7 on ALE and 29.9 on WildClaw, outperforming its 1K-trajectory counterpart by 13.0 and 8.4 points, respectively. At this scale, the model
also surpasses DeepSeek V3.2 (26.3) and MiniMax M2.7 (28.4) on ALE, as well as DeepSeek V3.2
(28.2) on WildClaw. Increasing data volume exposes the model to a broader range of workspace
structures, cross-file dependencies, and execution paths. Broader coverage promotes transferable
capabilities for workspace exploration and deliverable production.
Scaling with Interaction Horizon. Beyond data volume, we explore how interaction horizon
shapes agent behavior. We construct three subsets of 1K Claude Code trajectories, grouped by
trajectory length: 0–20 turns (G1), 21–40 turns (G2), and 41–60 turns (G3). As shown in Figure 4 (b), performance increases with trajectory length on both benchmarks. The model trained on
G3 achieves scores of 17.5 on ALE and 27.8 on WildClaw, exceeding its G1 counterpart by 4.2
and 5.0 points, respectively. This improvement is accompanied by more sustained interaction with
the environment. On WildClaw, the G3-trained model averages 24.0 productive tool calls, compared
with 11.7 for the G1-trained model, and performs 38.3 distinct actions, compared with 17.0. Table 13
further shows that the G3-trained model uses a broader range of tool types and invokes workspace
skills more frequently. These results suggest that longer training trajectories expose the model to
more complete sequences of dependency tracing, evidence gathering, and deliverable construction,
thereby promoting the sustained interaction required in complex workspaces.
3.5

B EHAVIOR A NALYSIS

We analyze the trajectory behavior of W ORK F ORGE-27B. Figure 6 (a) reveals a shift toward exploration behavior: the fine-tuned model visits 6.52 external web domains per task (5.3× the base
model’s average) and lifts targeted evidence retrieval from 5.9% to 16.2% of tool calls. After a
failed tool call, reasoning length grows from 144 to 614 characters (over fourfold), reflecting deliberate diagnosis and replanning rather than immediate retries. These shifts carry through to final
deliverables: Figure 6 (b) shows category-score gains of 4.5–13 points across five GDPval-derived
categories. Consistent with this shift toward exploration behavior, the largest gains appear in formatting and visual presentation (+13 pp) and numerical and calculation accuracy (+8.2 pp), which
rely on thorough investigation and careful verification.

R ELATED W ORK

Agent Environment Synthesis. Automated environment construction expands the supply of executable settings for agent training. EnvScaler and Agent World Model synthesize tool interfaces
and stateful backends (Song et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026a), while Agent-World incorporates realservice metadata and external resources (Dong et al., 2026). Skill and intent-driven pipelines construct tasks and supporting workspaces for terminal and general-assistant workflows (Cheng et al.,
2026; Bai et al., 2026). NexForge compiles capability requirements into task directives, then retrieves or constructs the resources needed for execution (Zhao et al., 2026). W ORK F ORGE instead
instantiates and profiles realistic workspaces before specifying concrete tasks. Preserving repository and dataset organization grounds professional tasks in heterogeneous materials, existing file
relationships, and observable evidence.
Long-Horizon Agent Data Synthesis. Agentic data synthesis expands multi-step execution
through tool interactions (Prabhakar et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025), task composition (Shi et al.,
2025; Xie et al., 2025), and exploration-based task discovery (Ramrakhya et al., 2025; Chen et al.,
2026; Wang et al., 2026b). Nemotron-Terminal studies generation, filtering, and long-context
training (Pi et al., 2026), while OpenThoughts-Agent examines task sources and diversity to train
OpenThinkerAgent-32B (Raoof et al., 2026). Executable-state alignment (Shi et al., 2026) and
rubric-based artifact assessment (Starace et al., 2025; Zhuge et al., 2025) connect trajectory quality
to task outcomes. W ORK F ORGE generates long-horizon supervision through sustained exploration,
cross-file reasoning, and artifact production in content-rich workspaces. Task requirements and verification criteria share the same workspace evidence, enabling complementary programmatic and
semantic evaluation to select trajectories for agent post-training.

5

C ONCLUSION , L IMITATIONS AND F UTURE W ORK

Conclusion We introduce W ORK F ORGE, a scalable framework for constructing complex, verifiable work-agent environments from real-world resources. Its core principle is to use observable
workspace facts as shared anchors for task construction and outcome verification. Starting from
expert workflows, W ORK F ORGE retrieves and profiles real-world resources, then derives task specifications, solution plans, and complementary programmatic and semantic verifiers from the resulting factual anchors. The pipeline instantiated 16.7K environments across 40 professional domains,
which improves the capability of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B-Base, including gains of 28.1 points on GDPval and 16.3 points on APEX. Our analysis indicates significant gains with increasing data volume
and trajectory length, while confirming the efficacy of verification mechanism.
Limitations and Future Work W ORK F ORGE depends on expert workflows and accessible realistic resources, limiting comparable grounding in proprietary or sensitive domains. Because
workspace profiles, tasks, and verifiers are constructed automatically, their reliability depends on
the completeness and accuracy of workspace profiling. Moreover, the current verifiers emphasize
final deliverables, receive limited expert calibration, and do not directly assess intermediate agent
behavior. Future work should broaden workflow and resource coverage while developing verification that is aware of processing.

T RAINING I NFRASTRUCTURE AND DATA F ORMATTING

Training uses the verl supervised fine-tuning trainer with Ray 2.46, PyTorch 2.7.1, CUDA 12.8,
Transformers 5.10.4, and FlashAttention 2.8.1. We use FSDP with Ulysses sequence parallelism of
size four. The Qwen3.5-35B-A3B-Base runs use 64 NVIDIA H20 96GB GPUs, and the Qwen3.527B runs use 128 GPUs. Optimizer states are offloaded to CPU, while model parameters remain on
GPU.
Training examples are rendered incrementally from cumulative conversation prefixes so that the
reasoning content associated with each assistant turn is preserved. The loss is applied according
to the per-message assistant loss mask stored with each trajectory. Variable-length examples are
packed without padding, with sequence boundaries retained through cumulative sequence-length
indices. Dynamic batching limits each GPU batch by its token count, with a maximum budget of
262,144 tokens.
