# Post-training evolution overview (2022-2026)

This overview synthesizes 99 paper digests drawn from `../texts/`, grouped by the year encoded in each arXiv ID. Years are corpus dates, not a publication-status claim; the 2026-dated entries are dated by their arXiv identifier and treated as part of the corpus regardless of real-world availability. The thesis is that post-training's goal migrated through three eras. In 2022–2023 it aimed to make pretrained LLMs *talk like desired assistants* — aligning surface style and instruction-following through synthetic data and human/AI preferences. By 2024 it aimed to make them *reason correctly* on verifiable domains (math, code) by replacing learned reward models with rule-based verifiers (RLVR) and distilling long chain-of-thought. From 2025 into 2026 it aims to make them *act competently over long horizons* — training agents in sandboxes and self-play with execution feedback, then compressing expensive reasoning into latent or budgeted forms. The binding constraint migrated in lockstep: from human-label cost, to coverage of open-ended behavior, to credit assignment and serving cost.

## Executive view

- **2022–2023 — surface alignment.** Goal: instruction-following plus preference alignment. Constraint: the cost and opacity of human annotation. Self-Instruct, LIMA, UltraChat/UltraFeedback and DPO showed synthetic data and a closed-form preference loss could rival RLHF products, exposing that style alignment did not fix correctness or grounding.
- **2024 — efficient preference + first verifiable rewards.** Goal: cheaper preference alignment and the first reasoning RL grounded in verifiers. Constraint: reward-model quality and PPO instability. The DPO family (KTO/ORPO/SimPO), RLVR (Tulu3), GRPO (DeepSeek-V3) and veRL infra replaced learned rewards with simpler or verifiable signals, exposing that static offline data could not cover open-ended agent behavior.
- **2025 — scalable reasoning and agentic RL.** Goal: RLVR for reasoning at scale plus the first real-world agentic RL. Constraint: the data and serving cost of long chain-of-thought, and credit assignment inside RLVR. DeepSeek-R1's pure-RL R1-Zero recipe, the GRPO-variant wave (DAPO, Dr. GRPO, PRIME, λ-GRPO), entropy correctives, SWE-RL, and self-improvement loops showed that final-answer rewards give weak credit assignment and that large reasoning models are costly to serve.
- **2026-dated — autonomous, environment-grounded agents + efficient reasoning.** Goal: agent training in synthetic sandboxes and self-play, plus compressed reasoning. Constraint: infrastructure and evaluation (sandbox rollouts, contamination, reward hacking). CUDA-Agent, Polar, SWE-Fuse, SandMLE, SSR, R³, SOAR, ReSyn, LTF, LaTER, and GRPO-VPS push difficulty away from data and onto infra and evaluation.

## Data curation: from volume to evidence quality

The older trend was synthetic expansion: Self-Instruct grew 175 seeds into 52K self-generated instructions; WizardLM's Evol-Instruct mass-produced complexity-controlled prompts; UltraChat distilled 1.47M multi-turn dialogues; UltraFeedback scaled GPT-4 AI feedback to a million preferences. Against this sat the small-data quality line — LIMA's 1,000 curated pairs, DEITA's score-first diversity-aware selection of 6K from a 300K pool — arguing alignment is "superficial" so a tiny high-quality set suffices. The two were reconciled by compute-adaptive curation: ScalingLawsFiltering showed the optimal filtering aggressiveness shifts with compute, and DataComp-LM systematized model-based quality filtering at 240T-token scale, finding filter choice swings MMLU by ~9 points.

The newer trend curates from execution and verifiers rather than stylistic preference. ACECODER auto-synthesizes test cases at scale to ground code reward models; SWE-RL mines 24M GitHub PRs as software-evolution data with rule-based rewards; ToolACE synthesizes 26.5K APIs with dual-layer verification; Tulu3 formalizes RLVR verifiers; ReSyn replaces (Q,A) pairs with (Q,verifier) pairs by having the LLM write code that *checks* a solution; SelfVerifiedDistillation filters self-generated candidates with prompt-based verification, needing no teacher, tools, or ground truth. The motivation is threefold: human labels are costly (the original Self-Instruct/LIMA argument); preference data optimizes style, not correctness, so it cannot ground reasoning or tool use; and benchmark contamination (SWE-Bench-Pro's GPL/copyleft sourcing, DCLM's pre-2023 cutoff) forces training and evaluation data toward freshly generated, verifier-checkable problems.

## Training curriculum: from one SFT stage to iterative loops

The older curriculum was a single linear sequence: SFT then preference/RL, mirroring InstructGPT's three stages. Zephyr ran dSFT→dDPO; Llama3 ran SFT→rejection sampling→DPO across six iterative rounds; Qwen2.5 ran SFT→offline DPO→online GRPO. The simplest variants were pure SFT (Self-Instruct, LIMA) or pure online RL on the same SFT data (ReFT).

The modern curriculum is explicitly multi-stage and often self-referential: seed→SFT→preference→RL on verifiable tasks→replay/filter→distill, with self-play loops layered on top. DeepSeek-R1 uses cold-start SFT→GRPO RL→reject-sampling SFT→RL; Tulu3 is curation→SFT→DPO→RLVR; DeepSeek-V4 runs SFT+GRPO specialists then consolidates them with on-policy distillation; Kimi K1.5 is pretrain→SFT→long-CoT SFT→RL→long2short transfer. Self-improvement loops thread through: STaR bootstraps rationales from answer correctness; RFT rejection-samples correct CoT paths; Self-Rewarding folds an LLM-as-judge reward model into the policy across iterative DPO; OpenSIR generates and solves problems in a verifier-free self-play GRPO loop from a single trivial seed; SSR makes one policy alternate bug-injector and bug-solver roles; SOAR builds a stepping-stone curriculum for problems a model cannot yet solve. Replay appears in R³'s cross-context replay and AgentGym/AgentTrek's trajectory reuse. The rationale: offline data cannot cover the combinatorial state space of open-ended agent behavior, mixing prevents regressions (AgentTuning blends agent and general data; Tulu3 resumes each stage from the prior checkpoint), and pure SFT plateaus where R1-Zero-style RL from a strong base elicits emergent reasoning that SFT cannot.

## Post-training methods: the main technical evolution

**(1) Preference optimization.** DPO (2023) derived the KL-constrained optimum in closed form and collapsed reward-model-plus-RL into one cross-entropy loss. IPO generalized this under ΨPO and diagnosed DPO's overfitting under deterministic preferences. KTO reframed alignment via prospect theory, enabling learning from non-pairwise binary feedback and skipping SFT at scale. ORPO made alignment monolithic and reference-free by adding an odds-ratio term to the SFT loss. SimPO replaced DPO's log-ratio reward with a length-normalized average-log-probability reward matching generation, cutting runtime and memory. The shared motivation: PPO RLHF is unstable and expensive, the frozen reference model is costly, DPO's reward is mismatched to decoding, and pairwise preferences are hard to collect.

**(2) Outcome and process reasoning RL.** STaR bootstrapped CoT from answer correctness; RFT replaced trained verifiers with rejection-sampling deduplication; Math-Shepherd automated step-level PRM labels via MCTS completion and plugged them into step-by-step PPO. GRPO (DeepSeekMath) dropped the critic and estimated advantages from group-relative rewards, becoming the RLVR workhorse. The 2025 variants attacked GRPO's pathologies: DAPO decoupled clipping, added dynamic sampling, and removed the KL penalty; Dr. GRPO diagnosed and removed length and difficulty biases; PRIME repurposed an outcome-trained ORM as an online-updatable implicit PRM; λ-GRPO unified GRPO/DAPO/Dr.GRPO under a learnable token-preference weight; GRPO-VPS added verifier-free process supervision via entropy cutpoints; PositiveAdvantageReweighting mitigated entropy collapse with per-token loss reweighting. The motivation: human PRM labels (PRM800K) do not scale, final-answer rewards give weak credit assignment, and GRPO's normalization biases inflate length and collapse exploration entropy.

**(3) Agentic and environment RL.** AgentTuning mixed multi-task agent-trajectory SFT with general data; SWE-agent showed that agent-computer interface design, not weight changes, drove coding-agent gains. WebRL added an off-policy actor-critic with a self-evolving curriculum; SWE-RL applied GRPO to GitHub-PR rule-rewards without execution feedback; RAGEN extended RL to multi-turn StarPO and surfaced the Echo Trap instability. The 2026 sandbox/self-play cluster then removes human-curated tasks entirely: CUDA-Agent runs execution-rewarded agentic RL for CUDA kernels; Polar treats any agent harness as a black-box rollout service; SWE-Fuse learns from issue-free trajectories with entropy-aware RLVR; SandMLE builds synthetic micro-environments to cut MLE rollout latency 13.7×; SSR plays bug-injector against bug-solver over real repos; R³ fixes GRPO's intra-group advantage collapse with cross-context replay; SOAR uses asymmetric self-play for stepping-stone curricula; ReSyn autonomously synthesizes code verifiers. The motivation: static trajectories cannot cover open-ended behavior, behavior cloning from all-correct experts cannot recover from errors (Agent-R), and execution feedback is the reliable but costly signal that self-play can generate for free.

**(4) Self-improvement and distillation.** Self-Rewarding folds the reward model into the policy so it co-improves across DPO iterations. OpenSIR bootstraps a verifier-free self-play curriculum from "What is 1+1?" with a diversity reward that nearly doubles concept coverage. DataEfficientDistillation uses teacher smoke-tests and entropy-aware corpus filtering so ~0.8K curated examples beat both teacher models and large-corpus baselines. SelfVerifiedDistillation turns unlabeled seed questions into supervised data via prompt-based self-verification. LTF replaces explicit CoT with variable-length continuous latent trajectories trained as GFlowNet reward-proportional matching over accuracy and cost. LaTER splits labor: latent exploration for search, explicit CoT for verification, reusing the latent KV cache. The motivation: human feedback has a ceiling, distillation from closed o1/R1 is limited, and serving large reasoning models is costly, so reasoning must be compressed into latent or shorter explicit forms.

**(5) Systems and multimodality.** veRL/HybridFlow provided a hierarchical hybrid programming model with pluggable reward modules that enabled the GRPO/RLVR wave. The frontier reports chart the same arc: Llama3 (SFT/RS/DPO), Qwen2.5 (DPO→GRPO), DeepSeek-V3/V3.2/V4 (GRPO, sparse attention, on-policy-distillation consolidation, native 1M context), Kimi K1.5/K2/K3 (online mirror descent, MuonClip, partial rollouts), GLM-4.5/GLM-5 (expert iteration + self-distillation), Gemma2/3 (distillation pretraining + RL), Phi-4 (Pivotal Token Search), Qwen3 (unified think/no-think with a thinking budget), Qwen3.5-Omni (ARIA streaming alignment), and GLM-5V-Turbo (CogViT + multimodal multi-token prediction). The motivation: RL infra must support pluggable verifiers and async rollouts, and multimodal agents need perception to be foundational rather than a post-hoc adapter.

## Evaluation and metrics: what changed

Evaluation began with static benchmarks, perplexity, human study, and model-judge win-rates: the Open LLM Leaderboard (ARC, HellaSwag, MMLU, TruthfulQA), MT-Bench and AlpacaEval with GPT-4 as judge, and the blinded human pairwise studies of LIMA and Zephyr. The math/code turn introduced verifiable pass@k and avg@k: pass@k on APPS/MBPP/HumanEval (CodeRL, RLTF), maj1@k on GSM8K (RFT), best-of-N weighted by PRM/ORM (OmegaPRM, the test-time-compute study), and avg@32 on AIME (DeepSeek-R1, DAPO) — avg@k rewarding consistency where pass@k rewards any success. The agentic turn introduced task completion and trajectory efficiency: SWE-bench (SWE-agent), WebArena (WebRL, AgentTrek), MultiPL-E, and progressively harder SWE variants — SWE-Bench-Pro, SWE-Fuse resolve rate, Terminal-Bench, tau2-Bench, BrowseComp, KernelBench (CUDA-Agent), and MLE-bench (SandMLE).

Metric validity itself became a research target. Contamination drove SWE-Bench-Pro to GPL/copyleft repos and DCLM to a pre-2023 cutoff. Reward hacking motivated InternLM2's multi-round online patching and RSO's rejection-sampling regularization. Verbosity inflation was diagnosed by Dr. GRPO's length-bias analysis and addressed by SimPO's length normalization and DAPO's overlong filtering. Evaluator bias (GPT-4-judge drift, UltraFeedback's annotator effects) pushed toward verifier-based scoring. Most starkly, the offline-vs-real gap widened: SWE-Bench-Pro shows top models dropping from >70% on SWE-Bench Verified to ~23% on Pro, and removing human augmentations collapses performance (GPT-5 25.9%→8.4%).

## Most recent technology evolution and its rationale (2025-2026)

The newest cluster coalesces around four moves. **(a) Verifiable/executable feedback** — ACECODER, SWE-RL, ReSyn, CUDA-Agent, SWE-Fuse, and SandMLE ground rewards in compilation, tests, or execution rather than learned reward models. **(b) Long-horizon agent trajectories** — RAGEN's StarPO, CUDA-Agent's 150-step PPO, Polar's async rollouts, Kimi K3's partial rollouts, and DeepSeek-V4's native 1M-token context extend credit assignment over hundreds of turns. **(c) Self-generated curriculum via replay, reflection, and self-play** — OpenSIR's diversity-rewarded problem generation, SOAR's asymmetric self-play for fail@128 problems, SSR's bug-inject/solve self-play, R³'s replay/reflection/ranking, ReSyn's autonomous environment synthesis, SelfVerifiedDistillation's self-verification, and SICA's non-weight scaffolding self-improvement. **(d) Efficiency via relative advantages, latent/process supervision, and distillation** — GRPO-VPS's entropy-cutpoint process supervision, PositiveAdvantageReweighting's entropy-collapse fix, λ-GRPO's learnable token weights, the GRPO U-statistic theory's group-size scaling law, LTF's latent thought flow, LaTER's latent-then-explicit reasoning, Mid-Think's token-level triggers, Think-with-Me's test-time intervention, and the distillation works (SelfVerifiedDistillation, DataEfficientDistillation).

These were proposed because human labels do not scale (R1-Zero pure RL, OpenSIR, SelfVerifiedDistillation without a teacher), because static datasets cannot cover agent behavior (SSR, SWE-Fuse, SandMLE, RAGEN), because final-answer rewards give weak credit assignment (PRIME, GRPO-VPS, R³, PositiveAdvantageReweighting all target entropy or intra-group advantage collapse), and because large reasoning models are costly to serve (LTF, LaTER, Mid-Think, Think-with-Me, Llama-Nemotron's thinking toggle, Qwen3's thinking budget). The trade-off is that difficulty shifts to infrastructure and evaluation: sandbox rollout services (Polar, MegaFlow in Qwen3-Coder-Next, SandMLE's latency engineering) and contamination-resistant benchmarks (SWE-Bench-Pro, GLM-5V's multimodal agent suites) become the new bottleneck, while reward hacking and training instability (SSR's flagged risks, RAGEN's Echo Trap) persist.

## Recommended reading path

Read the 2022–2023 corpus (Self-Instruct, LIMA, DPO, STaR, Math-Shepherd, AgentTuning) for the foundations of synthetic data, data-quality-over-quantity, preference optimization, and self-improving reasoning. Then 2024 (the DPO family KTO/ORPO/SimPO; Tulu3 and DeepSeek-V3 RLVR; Llama3/Qwen2.5; veRL infra) for preference-optimization maturation and the RLVR emergence. Then 2025 (DeepSeek-R1, DAPO/Dr.GRPO/PRIME/λ-GRPO, SWE-RL, LIMO/s1, Kimi K1.5, Qwen3, GLM-4.5) for the reasoning-RL and agentic-RL explosion. Finally the 2026-dated cluster (CUDA-Agent, Polar, SWE-Fuse, SSR, R³, SOAR, ReSyn, LTF, LaTER, GRPO-VPS, SelfVerifiedDistillation, DeepSeek-V4, GLM-5, Kimi-K3) for the current frontier of environment-grounded self-play, latent reasoning, and efficient distillation.
